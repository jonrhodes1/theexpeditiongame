Project Sac Actun

February 2024  ·  Sistema Sac Actun, Quintana Roo  ·  Mexico

The year is 2024. Your team of six technical divers has assembled at Tulum in the Yucatan Peninsula. Your mission: 30 days of cave diving exploration in the previously unmapped northwestern extension of the Sistema Sac Actun - the longest underwater cave system in the world. The system runs beneath 370km of Yucatan jungle, with passages at depths of 4 to 38 metres, and air pockets that have trapped explorers for days.

Cave diving has killed more experienced technical divers than any other diving discipline. The three primary causes of death are identical across every fatality record: running out of gas, losing the guideline, and diver panic. Your team follows the rule of thirds - one third of gas for the outbound dive, one third for the return, one third as reserve. No exceptions. No optimism.

Dr. Reyes, your team's cave diving safety specialist, has mapped 12km of Sistema Sac Actun over 8 years. She knows the known passages cold. The unexplored northwestern extension is genuinely unknown - passages no light has ever touched, air pockets with unmeasured atmospheric content, silt floors that can eliminate visibility in seconds.

14 scenarios across 30 days. Several carry timers. Gas and guideline decisions cascade through every subsequent dive. Only teams with exceptional technical discipline and zero-ego decision-making will complete the project and surface safely.
