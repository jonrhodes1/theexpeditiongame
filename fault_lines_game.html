Karsin Megacity · Coastal Region · Earthquake Response

Karsin is fictional, but the pressures are real: fractured infrastructure, overwhelmed local services, outside teams arriving fast, and a population trying to survive the first 72 hours after a severe earthquake.

You are leading an international response team through four phases: Activation & Deployment, Arrival & Integration, Operations, and Consequence & Extraction.

The visible metrics now start as a single 100-point briefing picture: Team Cohesion 30, Team Fatigue 20, Local Trust 20, and Operational Discipline 30. Two hidden metrics track what the team cannot see clearly: situational awareness and failure cascade risk. Small errors accumulate. Breakpoints do not announce themselves in advance.

Cohesion drives the systemWhen cohesion drops, discipline drops with it. Fragmented teams stop following their own standards cleanly.
Fatigue is spent, not gainedFatigue points mostly go down by at least 1 on every decision. The number represents remaining capacity, not how tired people feel.
Trust is external permissionLocal trust rises when you work through local structures and falls when speed or force displaces legitimacy.
Discipline is execution qualityDecisions that weaken cohesion or compress judgement will usually cost discipline as a second-order effect.
