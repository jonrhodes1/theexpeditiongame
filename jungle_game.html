Operation Javari

March 2024  ·  Javari Valley, Western Amazon  ·  Brazil

The year is 2024. Your international research team has arrived at the edge of the Javari Valley Indigenous Territory - a 8.5 million hectare protected area in the western Amazon that contains more uncontacted indigenous groups than anywhere else on Earth. Your mission: 60 days of biodiversity survey and ecosystem mapping in primary rainforest that has never been formally documented.

You are travelling with Marcos, a 48-year-old FUNAI indigenous affairs specialist from Atalaia do Norte. His colleagues Bruno Pereira and Dom Phillips were murdered in this valley in 2022. Marcos knows the risks. He came anyway because the science matters. He also knows the forest in ways no training programme teaches.

The Amazon in March is deep wet season. Rivers rise 6 to 8 metres in days. The forest floor floods without warning. Malaria, dengue fever, leptospirosis, and parasitic infections are not hypothetical - they are statistical certainties over 60 days. Evacuation is 72 hours minimum by river and air. You brought medicines but the jungle decides what problems you face.

14 scenarios across 60 days. Some carry timers. Earlier choices cascade into later medical crises. Only teams with disciplined protocols and strong cohesion will complete the research objectives and leave the Javari Valley.
