Operation Shackleton

November 2023  ·  Berkner Island, Antarctica  ·  2,200km

The year is 2023. Your expedition team has landed on Berkner Island at the edge of the Filchner Ice Shelf. Your objective: complete the Trans-Antarctic traverse that Ernest Shackleton attempted and failed in 1914 - crossing from the Weddell Sea coast, ascending the Beardmore Glacier, crossing the polar plateau to the South Pole, then continuing 1,300km to the Ross Ice Shelf and McMurdo Sound. The greatest polar crossing on Earth.

In 1914, Shackleton's ship Endurance was trapped by pack ice before the expedition began. His team spent 22 months fighting for survival. In 1958, Vivian Fuchs finally completed the traverse with motorised vehicles. This expedition does it on skis and muscle.

November is the Antarctic summer - but summer here means -25°C on the plateau, katabatic winds that can exceed 150km/h, and a crevasse field on the Beardmore Glacier that has swallowed equipment and threatened lives in every major expedition that has crossed it. You have no guide - you carry Shackleton's journals and modern satellite imagery.

14 scenarios across 60 days. Some decisions carry a 60-second timer. Earlier choices shape later crises. Only teams with extraordinary physical and psychological resilience will stand at McMurdo Sound.
