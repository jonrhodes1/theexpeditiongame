Operation Polaris

March 2023  ·  Cape Columbia, Ellesmere Island  ·  770km

The year is 2023. Your international expedition team has gathered in Qausuittuq (Resolute Bay), one of Canada's most remote communities, 1,100 kilometres inside the Arctic Circle. Your objective: ski unsupported from Cape Columbia - the most northerly point of land in North America - to the Geographic North Pole. Nine hundred kilometres across the frozen Arctic Ocean.

Inspired by the 2019 Borge Ousland and Mike Horn expedition - which crossed the Arctic Ocean in total polar darkness and came within hours of not returning - your team is the third international group to attempt this route in a decade. You carry Tatuq, a 51-year-old Inuit guide from Resolute Bay with 22 years of polar expedition experience. He has seen teams make every possible mistake on these waters.

March means temperatures of -40°C or colder. Open leads. Pressure ridges. Polar bears. Ice drift that erases your progress as you sleep. And 24-hour darkness that dismantles human cognition in ways no training programme fully prepares you for. The Twin Otter lands at Cape Columbia at 0800. The ice begins now.

14 scenarios across 60 days. Three timed crises. Earlier choices shape later survival. Only teams with exceptional cohesion, cold discipline, and sound decision-making will reach 90°N.
