Operation Thesiger

November 2024  ·  Rub' al Khali, Saudi Arabia  ·  1,200km

The year is 2024. Your international geoscience team has assembled at Shaybah, the remote oil facility at the eastern edge of the Rub' al Khali - the Empty Quarter. Your objective: a 60-day crossing of 1,200km of the world's largest continuous sand desert, completing climate and geological sampling along a north-south traverse that has never been fully documented at ground level.

You travel with Hamad, a 54-year-old Bedouin from the Al Murra tribe whose ancestors crossed this desert by camel for a thousand years. Wilfred Thesiger described the Al Murra as the finest desert travellers in the world. Hamad's knowledge of water sources, dune navigation, and desert weather patterns is the difference between a completed expedition and a search-and-recovery operation.

November in the Empty Quarter is the least extreme month - temperatures peak at 40-44°C rather than the summer maximum of 56°C. But the desert in November still kills through dehydration, heat stroke, and navigational error. Sand storms erase landmarks in minutes. Water caches separated by 200km are the only supply points. One miscalculation on water consumption changes everything.

14 scenarios across 60 days. Some carry timers. Water management decisions cascade through every subsequent day. Only teams with exceptional desert discipline and psychological resolve will complete the crossing.
